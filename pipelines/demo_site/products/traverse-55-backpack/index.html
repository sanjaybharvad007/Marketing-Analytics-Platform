Pack fit starts with torso length, not height, so measure from the bony bump at the base of your neck to the top of your hip bones. A hip belt should carry around eighty percent of the load, leaving the shoulder straps to steady the pack rather than hold it up. Fifty to sixty litres suits most trips of two to five nights once your sleeping system is reasonably compact. Load heavy items close to your spine and between your shoulder blades to keep the pack from pulling you backwards.

Mesh back panels breathe well in summer but push the load away from your back, which you will notice with heavier loads. Check that hip-belt pockets are reachable with the pack on, because that is where snacks and a phone will live. A pack that carries well at ten kilograms can feel very different at eighteen, so test it with realistic weight. Every product we sell is tested by staff on real trips before it reaches the shelf.
