Tents

A backpacking tent is a balance between weight, weather protection and living space, and every design gives up a little of one to gain another. Freestanding tents pitch quickly on rock and sand, while trekking-pole shelters save weight by leaving the frame at home. Look at the floor dimensions rather than the stated capacity: two wide sleeping pads need at least 130 centimetres of width. Double-wall construction keeps condensation off your sleeping bag, which matters more than a few saved grams on a humid night.

A vestibule large enough for boots and a pack keeps the inner tent dry and gives you somewhere sheltered to cook in bad weather. Seam-sealed flies and a bathtub floor rated above 3,000 millimetres will handle most three-season storms. Practise pitching at home before a trip so the first attempt is not in the dark with cold hands. Every product we sell is tested by staff on real trips before it reaches the shelf.

If something does not work out, returns are free within sixty days, used or not. Our stores run free clinics on fitting, repair and trip planning throughout the year. Gear that lasts is the most sustainable gear, so we repair what we sell and take trade-ins on what you have outgrown. Check the weather forecast and trail conditions the day before you leave, and tell someone your route.

Pack the ten essentials on every outing, including a headlamp and an extra insulating layer. Members earn credit on every purchase and get early access to seasonal sales. Ask our team for advice by chat or in store; they have walked most of the trails you are planning. Sizing charts are a starting point, and a fitting in store or a video call with our team settles the rest.
